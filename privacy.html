Privacy.

Your agents, chats and code stay on your Mac. This page lists everything else: what leaves your Mac or this website, where it goes, and how to turn it off.

On your Mac

ctrl+all keeps its data in ~/.ctrlall on your Mac: chats, agent transcripts, settings, paired devices and the provider keys you save. We have no server copy of it, so we cannot read it, back it up or restore it.

The agents you run (Claude Code, Codex, Gemini and the others) send their requests straight to the AI provider account you set up. ctrl+all does not sit in between and does not get a copy. That provider's own privacy policy covers what the agent sends it.

The relay

Your phone reaches your Mac through a relay. Ours runs at relay.ctrlall.app on Railway. The messages are end-to-end encrypted on your devices, so the relay cannot read your chats, files, code or keys.

The relay does see some facts about each connection:

  • the IP addresses of your Mac and your phone while they are connected;
  • when a device connects and disconnects;
  • how many messages pass and how large they are;
  • a hash that tells your devices apart, and a hash that matches a pairing phone to the right Mac.

The relay code keeps these in memory for the life of a connection and writes none of them to disk. Railway, which hosts the relay, keeps its own platform logs. You can avoid our relay entirely: run your own.

Google sign-in on a paired phone

A person on a paired phone can sign in with Google, so that their messages show their name and photo. The phone gets a sign-in token from Google and sends it to your Mac over the encrypted connection. Your Mac checks the token with Google's public keys and keeps three things in ~/.ctrlall: the account's Google id, its name and its photo link. It does not keep the email address.

The sign-in only names the person. It gives no access: you still approve each device on the Mac and choose its role. ctrl+all asks Google only for the account's basic profile (OpenID). Google's privacy policy covers Google's side of the sign-in.

Usage counts

The app sends short usage events to ctrlall.app, for example one a day to show it is in use, and one when a chat starts. Each event has a random install id, the app version and a value from a fixed list. The full list is public. Events never contain prompts, replies, files, paths, names, email addresses or keys.

The events go to Cloudflare Workers Analytics Engine, which keeps them for three months and then drops them. We do not store your IP address with them. Some events may also be copied to Google Analytics. A copy carries the install id and no other identifier.

Usage counts are on by default. Turn them off in Settings, Reports, "Send usage counts", or set CTRLALL_DISABLE_ANALYTICS=1. Turning them off deletes the install id.

Crash reports and feedback

When ctrl+all fails, it can send a crash report to Sentry. A report holds technical details: the kind of error, where in ctrl+all's code it happened, and the app version and build. Reports leave out chats, file contents, credentials, error message text and file paths that could name you.

Crash reports are on by default for new installs. Turn them off in Settings, Reports, "Send crash reports". Once you turn them off, they stay off.

Feedback goes only when you write it and press send. The app shows you exactly what it will send first: your message, the app build and the part of the app you were in. It goes to Sentry too.

Updates

The app checks for a new version by downloading a small file from GitHub (namangoyal3/ctrlall-releases). The check sends no install id. GitHub sees your IP address, as it does for any download. The app installs an update only when its signature matches the ctrl+all release key.

The app also downloads the list of models each engine offers from this site (ctrlall.app/models.json), at most four times a day. The request carries no identifier. The list is kept on your Mac, so the app shows the last one it saw when it is offline.

This website

Google Analytics. Every page of ctrlall.app loads Google Analytics. Google uses cookies to count visits and to see the pages you open, your browser and device, and your approximate location. A tracker blocker stops it, and the site still works.

Download sign-in. The download button asks you to sign in with Google. Google then tells us your email address, your name and your Google id. We keep them in our store on Cloudflare and add your email address to our release-news list. We use your email address to give you the download and to send ctrl+all release news, nothing else. You can skip the sign-in: download from GitHub.

Waitlists. When you join a waitlist on this site, we add the email address you type to the same release-news list.

Site counts. The site counts a few events, such as a press of the download button, in Cloudflare Workers Analytics Engine. They hold no visitor id. Some may be copied to Google Analytics.

Pro payments

Dodo Payments runs the Pro checkout. Your card details go to Dodo, never to us. When you pay, Dodo sends us a notice of the payment. From it we use your email address, your payment or subscription id, the plan and the date you have paid through. Dodo's privacy policy covers the checkout.

We keep a licence record on Cloudflare: the licence id, your key, the plan, the Dodo id, the dates, and a salted hash of your email address. The record does not hold the address itself. We email your key to you through Resend, our email service.

Deleting your data

  • On your Mac: run ctrlall uninstall --purge. It deletes the app and ~/.ctrlall.
  • Usage counts: turn them off. That stops new events and deletes the install id on your Mac. The rows already sent keep their install id and drop out after Cloudflare's three-month retention.
  • Download sign-in, release news and licences: write to support@ctrlall.app from the address in question. We delete the record and remove you from the list.
  • AI providers: ask the provider whose account you set up. We never had that data.

Changes

When ctrl+all starts to handle data in a new way, we change this page first and update the date at the top.
